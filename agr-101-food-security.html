Food Security in Nigeria

AGR 101 · notes, 69 questions, 36 flashcards

1. What is food security?

Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical, social and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active, healthy life (FAO).

Food insecurity is the opposite. It can be chronic (long-term, from persistent poverty), transitory (sudden shock such as flood or price spike) or seasonal (the lean season before harvest). SDG 2 is Zero Hunger.

2. Four pillars (FAO)

PillarMeaning
AvailabilityEnough food physically present: production, imports, stocks
AccessAbility to obtain it: income, prices, markets, roads
UtilizationBody uses it well: safe food, clean water, sanitation, health, nutrition knowledge
StabilityAvailability, access and utilization hold at all times despite shocks

3. Agriculture’s link to food security

Agriculture means crop farming, livestock, forestry, fishery and wildlife. Its roles in development: employment, source of food, raw materials, government revenue, foreign exchange, market for manufactured goods, labour for industry.

Modern farming also includes hydroponics (growing without soil: aeroponics, aquaponics, fogponics, deep water culture).

A resource only counts as a resource once man has exploited it for his welfare. Natural resource types: forest, soil, water, animal, atmospheric, human.

5. Policies and programmes

Policy = statement of action and tool for agricultural development. Programme = plan with objectives, resources needed and stages of work.

  • Colonial: surplus extraction (raw materials to Britain). Farm Settlement Scheme, 1959.
  • 1960–66: export-led growth. Cocoa (West), groundnut (North), oil palm (East).
  • Military era (1966–1999): NAFPP 1972, Agricultural Development Projects 1974, Operation Feed the Nation 21 May 1976, Green Revolution April 1980, National Fadama Development Project (1990s).
  • 1999 onward: NEEDS (2004), National Special Programme on Food Security (NSPFS) (2001/2002), Root and Tuber Expansion Programme (RTEP, IFAD-funded), Agriculture Transformation Agenda 2011–2016, Agriculture Promotion Policy 2016–2020.
  • NATIP 2021–2025 (National Agricultural Technology and Innovation Policy), part of the post-COVID Economic Sustainability Plan.
Dates checked against official sources.
  • NEEDS: draft launched 15 March 2004; main version launched 29 May 2004. The 1999 in one of your files matches no source.
  • NSPFS: FAO–Nigeria agreement signed 11 May 2000; first phase launched Nov 2001 (some sources say Jan 2002); project period 2002–2006.
  • RTEP: IFAD-funded (US$36.09 million, 25 states); IFAD’s programme paper dates from 1999. Neither 2003 nor 2008 is an official launch date. Implementation ran through the 2000s.
If your lecturer gave a different date, use theirs in the exam.

6. NATIP objectives

  • Modernise agriculture with technology and innovation
  • Private sector participation in value chains
  • Food security: sustainable production and better nutrition for a growing population
  • Youth engagement
  • Climate-smart agriculture
  • Access to finance and investment for smallholders

7. Causes of food insecurity in Nigeria

  • Crude tools, low mechanization, high cost of modern technology
  • Inadequate capital and poor access to credit (loans, grants)
  • Farmer–herder clashes, banditry and insurgency that displace farmers
  • Land tenure problems
  • Climate change: floods, drought, erratic rain
  • Poor produce pricing and few agro-processing industries
  • Post-harvest losses: weak storage, transport and roads
  • Inflation and poverty, which hurt access
  • Population growth and import dependence

8. Policy gaps

  • No interaction between stakeholders
  • Weak policies; conflicting roles between programmes
  • Short duration of policies and programmes
  • Emphasis mainly on food and animal production
  • Delay, embezzlement, misappropriation, lack of funds
  • Inadequate extension and advisory services
  • Poor monitoring and evaluation

9. Solutions

  • Mechanization, irrigation (Fadama), improved seeds, fertilizer
  • Storage, processing and better roads to cut post-harvest loss
  • Security for farmers and clear land-use rules
  • Credit and insurance for smallholders
  • Strong extension services and honest monitoring
  • Climate-smart practices; youth in agribusiness

10. Why a medical student cares

Malnutrition shows up in the clinic: stunting (low height-for-age), wasting (low weight-for-height), kwashiorkor (protein deficiency with oedema), marasmus (severe energy deficiency, no oedema) and hidden hunger (micronutrient deficiency).

From your class notes, ways one person can help locally: crop farming, animal domestication, fruit farming, vertical farming, poultry, rabbit keeping and fish farming.
