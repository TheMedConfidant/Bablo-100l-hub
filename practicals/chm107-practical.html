Experiment 3 · Manual p. 15

Calibration of Pipette and Burette

Procedure (from manual)

  1. Weigh a clean, dry specimen tube and record its mass, x g.
  2. Discharge the specified volume (25 cm³ or 10 cm³, as directed) of distilled water from the burette into the pre-weighed tube.
  3. Weigh the tube plus water and record y g. Mass of water = (y − x) g.
  4. Repeat five times with the burette.
  5. Repeat the determination using the corresponding pipette volume.

Solution guide

Average mass of water = sum of measured water masses ÷ number of trials.

Actual volume (cm³) = average mass of water (g) ÷ density of water at the measured temperature (g cm⁻³).

Relative error (%) = |actual volume − nominal volume| ÷ nominal volume × 100.

For precision, calculate the standard deviation of the repeated volumes; lower spread indicates better repeatability. Use your recorded measurements—do not substitute made-up readings.

Experiment 3B · Manual p. 15

Calibration of a Thermometer

Procedure (from manual)

  1. Place the thermometer bulb in a well-stirred ice–water mixture for about five minutes.
  2. Record the thermometer reading at this fixed point.
  3. Place the thermometer for about five minutes in steam generated in a conical flask.
  4. Record the second reading and report both fixed-point readings to the instructor.

Solution guide

At standard atmospheric pressure, the reference points are approximately 0 °C for melting ice and 100 °C for steam. Error at each point = observed reading − reference reading. A correction can be estimated as reference reading − observed reading. The manual notes that an error exceeding 1 degree indicates possible systematic error.
Experiment 5B · Manual p. 21

Choice of Indicators

Aim: Determine a suitable indicator for different acid/base combinations.

Materials listed: Methyl orange, phenolphthalein, 0.1 M H₂SO₄, 0.1 M NaOH, 0.1 M Na₂CO₃, burette, pipette, conical flask and stand.

Procedure (from manual)

  1. Titrate the supplied sulphuric acid against sodium hydroxide using methyl orange; repeat three times.
  2. Repeat the acid–base titration using phenolphthalein.
  3. Titrate sulphuric acid with sodium carbonate in two separate trials: methyl orange for the first and phenolphthalein for the second.
  4. Compare the volumes and recommend a suitable indicator for each combination.

Solution guide

Compare concordant titres and the sharpness of the endpoint. For a strong acid–strong base titration, both indicators can show a useful endpoint because the pH changes steeply near equivalence. For H₂SO₄–Na₂CO₃, methyl orange is generally the more suitable endpoint indicator for complete neutralisation; phenolphthalein can indicate the carbonate-to-bicarbonate stage instead. Explain any titre difference by the indicator transition range and the reaction stage detected.
Experiment 6 · Manual p. 22

Acid–Base Titration: Strong Acid Against Strong Base

Manual procedure: Titrate the given 0.2 M NaOH with the supplied nitric acid solution using 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein. The endpoint is the change from pink to colourless when acid is in the burette. Repeat three times and average concordant readings (manual states readings within ±0.01 cm³).

Solution guide

Reaction: HNO₃ + NaOH → NaNO₃ + H₂O (1:1).

At equivalence: n(HNO₃) = n(NaOH) = C(NaOH) × V(NaOH in dm³).

C(HNO₃) = n(HNO₃) ÷ V(HNO₃ in dm³).

Mass concentration of HNO₃ (g dm⁻³) = molar concentration × 63.01 g mol⁻¹. Insert your actual mean titre and pipetted volume to obtain numerical answers.

Experiment 7 · Manual p. 23

Acid–Base Titration: Weak Acid Against Strong Base

Manual procedure: Titrate the supplied 0.1 M acetic acid (CH₃COOH) against 0.1 M NaOH using phenolphthalein as indicator. Record the titre.

Solution guide

Reaction: CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O (1:1).

n(CH₃COOH) = C × V in dm³. At equivalence, n(NaOH) = n(CH₃COOH).

C(NaOH) = n(NaOH) ÷ V(NaOH in dm³); mass concentration = C × 40.00 g mol⁻¹.

Phenolphthalein is suitable because the equivalence point for a weak-acid/strong-base titration lies above pH 7.

Experiment 8 · Manual p. 24–25

pH / Acid–Base Titration Curve

The scan shows a pH-versus-volume titration exercise followed by questions asking for a graph, equivalence point, and comparison with an indicator endpoint. The exact experiment heading and some first-page lines are faint in the scan.

Procedure guide: Measure and record the initial pH; add the titrant in measured increments, mixing after each addition and recording pH and total volume. Use smaller increments near the sharp pH change. Continue to the endpoint volume specified in the manual.

Solution guide: Plot pH on the vertical axis against total titrant volume (cm³) on the horizontal axis. Estimate the equivalence volume from the inflection/steepest section of the curve. Compare it with the indicator endpoint; a mismatch can result from the indicator's transition range.

Redox Titrations

Manual theory: Redox titrations involve electron transfer between titrant and titrand. Strongly coloured titrants such as potassium permanganate can be self-indicating; starch is used in iodine titrations.
